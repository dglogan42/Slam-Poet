# Loud Enough — Story Bible

## Premise

Beatrice "Bea" Santos, 16, is the reliable one: after school she works the register at her family's diner, translates for her grandmother, and watches her little brother while her mother runs the kitchen. Her father has been working construction in Dubai for three years, sending money home every month and missing everything. Bea has one thing that's only hers — a battered notebook of poems she performs under a fake name at a Thursday-night open mic across town. When she's accepted into the finals of a statewide teen poetry slam, the date collides with the diner's 20th-anniversary weekend, the biggest and most financially important night of her family's year. Bea has to decide whether she's allowed to choose herself, and whether her family can make room for the person she's been hiding from them.

## Themes

- The difference between being *needed* and being *seen*.
- Immigrant-family duty and love expressed through labor, not words.
- Finding your voice literally and figuratively — who gets to be loud.
- Inherited dreams: the parent's unlived life shaping the child's guilt.
- Growing up doesn't mean leaving your family; it means being known by them.

## Setting

Port Alden, a small coastal city (fictional, US Pacific Northwest-adjacent). The Santos family runs **Sampaguita Diner**, a Filipino-American diner near the harbor, open 6am–9pm, established by Bea's grandparents. Present day, contemporary. Story spans about six weeks in early autumn (September–October), junior year.

## Voice & Craft

- First person, past tense, Bea's POV throughout.
- Voice: dry, observant, tender under the deflection; code-switches lightly with Tagalog terms (*Lola, Ate, opo, salamat*) used the way a bilingual household actually would — untranslated in dialogue, meaning clear from context.
- Poems appear on the page as Bea's poems, in verse, interspersed where she writes or performs them.
- Chapters run roughly 1,800–2,600 words.

## Principal Characters

- **Beatrice "Bea" Santos** — 16, junior at Port Alden High. Sharp, funny in her own head, quiet out loud. Writes poetry at night. Believes love is something you earn by being useful.
- **Elena Santos** — Bea's mother, 42. Runs the diner's kitchen and books. Immigrated from Cebu at 19. Gave up a scholarship to study nursing to help her own parents' restaurant — a fact she's never told Bea outright.
- **Ramon Santos** — Bea's father, 45. Electrician working a three-year contract in Dubai. Present via Sunday video calls. Loves his family by wiring money; doesn't know how to say more than that on a bad connection.
- **Lola Corazon "Lola Cora" Bautista** — Bea's grandmother, 71, Elena's mother. Co-founded the diner. Speaks English in short, exact sentences and Tagalog in long, funny ones. Has a health scare (a minor stroke / TIA) partway through the book. Turns out to know more about Bea's secret than anyone.
- **Nico Santos** — Bea's brother, 10. Obsessed with a card game and with being included. A barometer for how much Bea has been running the house.
- **Priya Nair** — Bea's best friend since 7th grade. Knows about the notebook but not about the open mic. Debate team captain, wants Bea to actually let people clap for her for once.
- **Eli Cho** — 17, works the counter at Marlow's, the bookstore-café that hosts the Thursday open mic. Also a poet, quieter than Bea expects a boy who performs to be. Becomes Bea's first real relationship — not a rescue, a mirror.
- **Marlow's** — bookstore-café that hosts the weekly open mic where Bea performs as "B."
- **State Voices** — the statewide teen poetry slam; regional heat in Port Alden, finals in the state capital the same weekend as the diner's anniversary.

## Chapter Map

1. **Closing Shift** — Establish the diner, the family, Bea's competence and invisibility. She hides her notebook from her mother mid-shift.
2. **B at the Mic** — Thursday night at Marlow's. Bea performs anonymously as "B." Meets/reconnects with Eli.
3. **Sunday Call** — Family dinner, the weekly video call with her dad, Lola's habits, the shape of the household economy. Plants Nico's need for attention.
4. **The Flyer** — At school, Priya shows her the State Voices regional signup. Bea says no on reflex. Priya pushes.
5. **Eleven Lines** — Bea and Eli talk for real at Marlow's; her poetry becomes visible to one more person. She submits to the regional heat in secret.
6. **Twenty Years** — Elena announces the diner's 20th-anniversary weekend event; the whole family is drafted. Bea learns the date.
7. **The Regional Heat** — Bea competes (lying about where she is), wins a finals slot. Elation collides with the calendar conflict.
8. **What Lola Knows** — Small signs Lola has noticed the notebook / the late Thursdays. A quiet, layered conversation instead of an accusation.
9. **The Fall** — Lola has a health scare at the diner during a rush. Family crisis; Bea steps up completely, finals recede.
10. **What Elena Gave Up** — In the hospital waiting room, Elena tells Bea, for the first time, about the nursing scholarship she didn't take. Reframes "duty" as something that costs everyone, not just Bea.
11. **The Blowup** — Bea finally tells her mother about State Voices; it comes out sideways, in anger, during anniversary-weekend prep chaos.
12. **Sampaguita** — The repair. Elena, Lola, and Bea find a plan that doesn't require Bea to disappear from either room. Small concrete compromise, not a speech.
13. **Both Rooms** — The anniversary night and the finals are hours apart in the same city (a plan makes this barely possible); Bea performs at State Voices, family shows up in a way she didn't expect.
14. **Open Mic** — Weeks later, an ordinary Thursday. Bea performs at Marlow's under her own name for the first time, family and Eli in the room. Coda: the notebook is not a secret anymore.

## Continuity Notes

- Bea's slam alias: **"B."**
- Diner name: **Sampaguita Diner** (sampaguita = Philippine national flower, jasmine; symbol threaded through the book — Lola grows it in a window box).
- Dad calls every **Sunday, 7pm** Port Alden time (early Monday morning in Dubai).
- Regional heat: **Saturday, October 3**, Port Alden Branch Library. Bea wins, places into finals.
- Diner's 20th-anniversary night: **Saturday, October 24** — kicks off "anniversary season," extended hours through mid/late November.
- State Voices finals: **Saturday, November 7**, state capital (three hours away) — falls inside anniversary season, creating the chapter 11 conflict. Bea competes in the morning/early afternoon, is back in Port Alden for dinner rush. Places 2nd in state.
- Lola's health scare: a TIA ("mini-stroke") during Thursday dinner rush — frightening, recoverable, used to raise stakes without a death.
- Nico's card game: **Kaiju Clash**, a stand-in trading card game he's saving allowance for.
- Final word count: ~25,000 words across 14 chapters.
